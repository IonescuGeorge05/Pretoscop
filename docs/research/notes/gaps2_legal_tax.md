## 1. Law 506/2004 (ePrivacy): cookies, commercial communications, sanctions, and the "reject all" bill

### Takeaway
The current consolidated text (last consolidation 08.10.2024) contains the following:
- **Art. 4(5):** non-essential storage or access needs prior information plus consent, cumulatively.
- **Art. 4(5^1):** consent may also be given through browser settings.
- **Art. 4(6):** two exemptions, transmission and "strictly necessary".
- **Art. 12:** opt-in for e-mail and automated commercial communications.
- **Art. 13:** breach of art. 4(5) is letter **i)** and breach of art. 12 is letter **q)**. Both carry fines of **RON 5,000–100,000**, or **up to 2% of turnover** for companies with turnover above RON 5 million. ANSPDCP enforces.
- **ANSPDCP fines in 2026:** RON 5,000 and RON 30,000 for cookies, and RON 50,000 for automated calls.
- **The "reject all" bill:** L256/2026 is now **Pl-x 455/2026** at the Chamber of Deputies, which is the deciding chamber. The Senate rejected it on 2 June 2026 and the Government's view is negative. The Chamber's joint committees filed a **favourable report with 6 amendments on 20 Oct 2026**, and the bill is **on the plenary agenda for 26 and 28 Oct 2026**.

### Inferences
- **What the current law requires:** prior information plus consent, cumulatively, with only two exemptions. It does not literally say "reject all on the first layer". ANSPDCP's 2026 cases show it fines small sites for non-essential cookies set before consent, from RON 5,000 to 30,000. Keep the planned design: no non-essential tags before consent, and equal Accept/Reject on the first layer.
- **If Pl-x 455/2026 passes on 28 Oct:** the Chamber decides, so it would go straight to promulgation, subject to a possible CCR challenge or re-examination request. The 6 committee amendments may change the content and the 120-day transition reported for the initiator's version.
- **Price alerts:** e-mails or push messages promoting retailers' offers are "comunicări comerciale". They need prior express opt-in under art. 12(1). The art. 12(2) soft opt-in assumes a "sale … of a product or service". Whether a free account counts as one turns on CJEU *Inteligo Media* (C-654/23), which was not re-read (see Gaps).

## 2. Law 363/2007 (unfair commercial practices) after OUG 58/2022, and HG 947/2000 (the 30-day prior price)

### Takeaway
- **Where OUG 58/2022 placed the UCPD rules in Law 363/2007:**
  - art. 7(4)(f): the marketplace trader-status disclosure;
  - **art. 7(4^1)**: the main ranking parameters, with a carve-out in **art. 7(4^2)** for online search engines;
  - **art. 7(6)**: reviews;
  - **Annex 1, "Practici comerciale înșelătoare" (misleading practices), points 24, 25, 26 and 27**, which are UCPD Annex I points 11a, 23a, 23b and 23c.
- These are in force since **28.05.2022**.
- **Fines for misleading practices, including Annex 1:** **RON 20,000–100,000**, doubled where more than 100 consumers are affected. For widespread infringements under Reg. 2017/2394 the fine is up to 4% of Romanian turnover.
- **HG 947/2000, as amended by HG 686/2022:** **art. 4^1** sets the lowest-price-in-30-days rule, and **art. 12(2)** sets a fine of **RON 5,000–30,000** for breaching it.

### Inferences
- **Ranking disclosure:** Pretoscop lets consumers search products "oferite de diferiți comercianți … indiferent de locul în care se încheie tranzacțiile" (*offered by different traders, regardless of where the transactions are concluded*), so art. 7(4^1) applies. It needs a "Cum ordonăm rezultatele" ("how we rank results") section linked directly from every results page.
- **Paid ranking:** if affiliate commission, CPC or any payment influences position, Annex 1 pct. 24 requires clear disclosure next to the results. This is a blacklist item, so no materiality test applies.
- **Reviews:** if Pretoscop ever displays reviews, art. 7(6) as worded in Romania arguably requires access to *all* reviews, plus a statement of whether and how they are verified.
- **Marketplace rule:** Pretoscop does not let consumers conclude contracts, so it is not a "piață online" under art. 2 lit. o). The art. 7(4)(f) duty therefore attaches to the marketplaces (OLX, eMAG Marketplace) rather than to Pretoscop. Showing the platform's trader/consumer flag is still good practice.
- **HG 947/2000 does not bind Pretoscop directly:** the duty falls on the seller announcing the reduction, and the text is framed around physical sales areas. Online application rests on ANPC practice noted in earlier notes. If Pretoscop computes its own "−X%" badges, they are Pretoscop's own claims under Law 363/2007 arts. 6–7. Computing the reduction against the lowest price in the last 30 days, as art. 4^1 does, is the safe convention.

### Inferences
- **Price and specification data:** prices and specs are not works, so the relevant protection is the retailer's database right (arts. 140–143). Extracting individual prices product by product is extraction of insubstantial parts, which art. 142(1) permits for a lawful user. Systematic, repeated full-catalogue crawling is what art. 141(5) targets. CJEU *CV-Online Latvia* (C-762/19) adds a harm-to-investment test (earlier notes).
- **Art. 36^2 may not help:** the general TDM exception mentions "opere și … alte obiecte protejate" (*works and other subject matter*) but, unlike art. 36^1(2), not art. 141(1). DSM Directive art. 4(1)(a) covers Database Directive art. 7(1) rights, so a Romanian court could read art. 36^2 consistently with the Directive. This is untested. In any case, honour machine-readable opt-outs: robots.txt, TDM-Rep and site-level reservations.
- **Photos versus data:** prices and specifications are "simplele fapte și date" (art. 9 lit. f)), outside copyright, so only the database right can protect them. Retailer and manufacturer product photos are normally photographic works under art. 7 lit. f). Hot-linking or re-hosting them needs a licence, for example through an affiliate feed's terms, or an exception. Hosting Pretoscop's own crawl of photos is the riskiest option.
- **Brand names:** using brand names in product titles to identify goods is covered by art. 45(1)(c) when done honestly. Logos or layouts that suggest partnership are not.

## 5. Company and founder taxes: micro D100 at zero revenue, CASS on dividends for salaried founders, CASS cap, minimum-wage payroll, micro rules after OUG 8/2026

### Takeaway
- **D100 at zero revenue:** a micro-enterprise files D100 **every quarter by the 25th of the following month even when revenue is zero**. The Code makes filing unconditional (art. 56(2)), and the D100 instructions say to enter "0".
- **Salaried founder and dividends:** a salary does **not** exempt a founder from **CASS on dividends**. Dividends count net of the 16% tax toward the 6/12/24 minimum-wage thresholds. Salaried persons are exempt only for IP income.
- **CASS cap on PFA income:** raised to **72 minimum wages by Law 239/2025** (Art. XII pt. 19), for income from 2026.
- **New-company micro grace period:** now **90 days** to hire the required employee (art. 48(3)).
- **Payroll at the minimum wage in H1 2026** (RON 4,050, with the RON 300 untaxed amount), computed from the Code's rates: **net about RON 2,574**, **employer cost about RON 4,134**.

## 6. VAT and invoicing: art. 317 code, D301, D390, SAF-T for new companies, self-billing, Awin/Admitad/Google, e-Factura, individuals receiving payouts

### Takeaway
- **Art. 317 VAT code:** requested on **form 091** (OPANAF 631/2016 art. 1 lit. a) **before** the first intra-EU B2B service is supplied or received. The registration is valid from the date of the request.
- **D301:** reverse-charged services bought by the company go on the special VAT return **D301** by the 25th of the following month.
- **D390:** monthly by the **25th of the following month**, using symbols **P** (services supplied) and **S** (services received). UK operations are excluded.
- **SAF-T D406 for a new non-VAT SRL:** **quarterly**, due by the last day of the following month. The **first report has a 3-month grace period** (OPANAF 1783/2021).
- **Self-billing by EU networks:** the invoicing rules of the customer's member state apply; for Awin AG that is German law. Romanian norms (pt. 96) apply only to self-billing by non-EU customers.
- **Awin:** contracts with EU-resident publishers through **AWIN AG (Berlin)** and self-bills.
- **Admitad:** has a German-entity version and a "Global" (Dubai) version of its terms, and no self-billing clause.
- **Google AdSense:** issues only a **payment receipt**, so the SRL must issue its own reverse-charge invoice.
- **Law 12/1990:** **repealed in 2020**, so its fines for unregistered activity no longer exist.

## 8. Scraping and contract law: Civil Code standard clauses, "acces fără drept", Romanian case law, platform terms (OLX, Publi24, Altex, PC Garage), affiliate-network terms, foreign precedents

### Takeaway
- **Civil Code art. 1203** makes only a **closed list** of standard clauses ineffective unless expressly accepted in writing: limitation of liability, unilateral termination, suspension, forfeiture of rights, limits on raising defences, restrictions on contracting with others, tacit renewal, applicable law, arbitration and jurisdiction clauses. A "no scraping" clause is not on the list. The open question is whether a crawler ever accepted the terms at all (art. 1202).
- **Criminal law:** art. 360 of the Criminal Code punishes access to a computer system "fără drept" (*without right*). Law 161/2003 art. 35(2) defines "fără drept" as including access not authorised "în temeiul legii sau al unui contract" (*by law or by contract*).
- **No Romanian court decision on scraping or comparison sites was found.** The only Supreme Court referral on art. 360 (ÎCCJ Decision 68/2021) was dismissed as inadmissible. The court databases ReJust and rolii.ro cannot be queried automatically.
- **Platform terms:**
  - Publi24 restricts use of content to personal, non-commercial use and calls unauthorised server access a criminal offence;
  - Altex's May 2026 terms contain only a general IP clause, with no robot clause found;
  - OLX's and PC Garage's terms could not be retrieved.
