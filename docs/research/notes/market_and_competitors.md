## 1. Market: size, growth, eMAG's weight, number of shops, mobile, peak events, delivery, payments

### Takeaway
How big the market looks depends on scope. B2C goods plus services was **€12.9bn in 2025 (+10%)**, forecast at **€13.5bn for 2026** (European E-Commerce Report 2026). Physical goods alone were about **€8.1bn in 2025 (+6%)**, forecast to pass €8.5bn in 2026 (+5%) (MerchantPro). Growth is slowing, and Temu and Trendyol are taking traffic from local shops. eMAG is still the hub: about 10.6bn lei of platform sales in 2025, more than 64,000 marketplace sellers, and a 986M-lei Black Friday on 7 Nov 2025. Romanian Black Friday runs early November (eMAG: **6 Nov 2026**). Lockers are expanding fast: Sameday easybox passed 6,000 and FANbox reached 3,000. Cash on delivery is still common, although urban online surveys show card as the most-used method.

### Inferences
- For planning, use **about €8–8.5bn for physical goods** and **about €13–13.5bn for B2C including services** in 2025–26. Growth is mid-single-digit, which means a comparator wins by taking share and SEO traffic, not by riding market growth.
- eMAG's 10.6bn lei of platform sales is about €2.1bn at roughly 5.0–5.1 RON/EUR. That is **about a quarter of the physical-goods market** (my calculation, approximate FX). Any comparator without full eMAG coverage, including marketplace sellers, will feel incomplete to users.
- Today is 27 Oct 2026, so eMAG's Black Friday (6 Nov) is 10 days away and evoMAG's campaign starts 29 Oct. **If scraping starts this week**, Pretoscop can capture pre-BF and BF 2026 prices. Even with only about 10 days of pre-BF history, that is enough to publish a "Black Friday 2026: which discounts were real" analysis, the kind PriceSpy and Which? use as PR (see §4/§5). A full Omnibus-style 30-day check would only be possible for later campaigns, from Christmas 2026 onwards.
- Locker data (easybox, FANbox) and COD/instalment options are useful offer attributes for Romanian shoppers. Merchant distrust is a top reason for COD (37%), so trust signals matter.

- No GPeC full-year 2025 report figure was found; one ProTV listing headline (27 May 2025) said the market had passed €8bn, but its scope was not confirmed ([ProTV listing](https://stirileprotv.ro/stiri-despre/ecommerce/)).

## 2. Retailers: top Romanian online shops by category (domains, marketplace status, traffic)

### Takeaway
Electronics and IT has the densest field of large, comparable shops: eMAG, Altex/Media Galaxy, Flanco, PC Garage, evoMAG, CEL, Vexio, ITGalaxy, Forit, iStyle and F64. Pharma, beauty/perfume, books and DIY each have 5–10 sizeable shops. Fashion and general merchandise are increasingly dominated by cross-border marketplaces (Trendyol, Temu, Zalando, Shein). ## 3. Romanian competitors: price-comparison, price-history and deal-tracking services

### Takeaway
**compari.ro (Heureka Group)** is the only scaled comparator, with about 3.6M visits a month, #1 in price comparison and 2,500+ merchants. It is turning itself into a **marketplace** (3–5.5% commission, launched July 2025). Every price-history specialist is small, under about 200K visits a month: istoric-preturi.info, pricy.ro, price.ro, xprice.ro and shopmania.ro (now a shadow of its 2009 self). Their browser extensions have only a few thousand users, and stale prices are the recurring complaint. Skroutz.ro (Greek marketplace/comparator, being acquired by Blackstone) and vertical comparators (Glami for fashion, Favi and Biano for furniture) are the other threats. **I found no Romanian service that combines full coverage, long and credible history, fake-discount verdicts and alerts at scale.**

### Inferences
- **Market gap:** the scaled player, compari, is moving towards checkout and commissions, which creates a perceived conflict of interest. The neutral price-history tools are small and have stale-data problems. A product built on *trustworthy, fresh history, Omnibus-aware discount verdicts and full eMAG marketplace coverage* has room. Distribution is the hard part: the incumbents' traffic is mostly organic search and direct.
- The existing price-history sites together get roughly 0.3–0.4M visits a month, about a tenth of compari. That suggests either latent demand that no one has served well, or a niche. The Black Friday and fake-discount PR angle (§5) is the main way to grow it.
- Monetization benchmarks available locally: merchant **PPC** (compari), **marketplace commission of 3–5.5%** (compari, against about 18% at competitors), and **affiliate** through the now-consolidated 2Performant/Profitshare network.
- The risk to watch is Skroutz.ro backed by Blackstone. It combines a comparator heritage, a marketplace, logistics and a loyalty programme (see §4).

### Gaps
- compari.ro's features (price-history chart? alerts? how far back?) could not be verified because the site returns 403. Check manually.
- price.ro's owner, launch year and features were not found. pretheure.com and preturireduse.ro were seen only as Similarweb similar sites.
- No Telegram or Facebook deal channels, and no "reduceri" aggregator traffic, were found. Searches returned only eMAG campaign news, and the search budget was then exhausted.
- **No Reddit (r/Romania, r/programare) threads were found.** The search tool returned no Reddit results, so user complaints are thin: Trustpilot has 3 reviews and the Firefox reviews are few.
- No Google Play or App Store data was retrieved for the pricy.ro apps.
- Price-history depth (how many months or years back) is not published by any Romanian competitor I checked.

## 4. International benchmarks and the 10 features that matter most

### Takeaway
The best services pair a **trustworthy price history** with **active verdicts**: Heureka's "verified discounts" and buy-now box, Google's low/typical/high and 30-day low, and PriceSpy's fake-sale studies. They add **alerts**, **merchant-trust programmes** (Heureka "Ověřeno zákazníky", Ceneo "Zaufane Opinie"), **total price including shipping** and **deep filters** (Geizhals). Business models split between pure PPC comparators (Geizhals, PriceRunner, idealo after it closed Direktkauf at end-2022) and comparators that became marketplaces (Skroutz, Ceneo "Kup Teraz", Heureka/compari). Skroutz is the best analogue for a smaller EU market.

### Inferences
- **Skroutz playbook** (comparator → marketplace → logistics → loyalty programme) is the outcome compari is chasing in Romania. Pretoscop should probably **stay a neutral comparator and verifier**, as idealo, Geizhals and PriceRunner did, and turn neutrality into the brand ("we don't sell, we check"). The Alza–Heureka split shows that neutrality also helps keep large merchants on board.
- Google shows price insights only in the US, so Romanian Google results have no "is this a good price?" layer. That is a gap Pretoscop can fill on SEO landing pages.
- An annual **"fake Black Friday discounts" report** is a proven PR engine (PriceSpy, Which?). Romanian press already writes about ANPC and reduceri false every November (§5).

### Gaps
- No public figures on PriceRunner's or PriceSpy's history depth, Árukereső's features, or Kelkoo were retrieved. Trovaprezzi and Ceneo traffic and revenue after 2022 were not found.
- Keepa's official pricing and user counts were not verified on keepa.com.
- No direct user survey ranking these features was found. The ranking above is my synthesis of the evidence.

## 5. Romanian consumer needs: demand for price history and fake-discount detection

### Takeaway
Romanians are among Europe's most discount-sceptical shoppers: 63% say brands cheat on BF, 83% track prices before BF, and 29% don't trust displayed discounts. ANPC enforces the 30-day lowest-price reference rule and has fined Altex and others. eMAG now advertises discounts against "CMMP 30" and the "lowest price of the year", while the PRP (manufacturer recommended price) display remains controversial. The demand signal is strong in surveys and press, but direct behavioural proof (search volumes, Reddit) was not obtained.

### Inferences
- The core need is a **neutral verdict** ("Is this discount real? Is this the lowest price in 30, 90 or 365 days, across all shops?") more than a list of prices. eMAG's own CMMP 30 labels cover only eMAG's own history. A cross-shop, cross-time verdict is something only an independent tool can provide.
- Omnibus compliance gives the planner a legal, defensible frame: compute each shop's own 30-day low from Pretoscop's history, and flag claimed discounts that don't hold. This needs at least 30 days of history per product, so **crawling must start immediately**.
- PRP-based "fake" discounts are an easy, high-visibility target for content and SEO ("PRP vs preț real").

## 6. Launch-category choice

### Takeaway
The evidence points to **electronics and IT (phones, laptops, monitors, TVs, components) plus small and large appliances** as the launch core. It has the most comparable shops, EAN-based listing at eMAG, the strongest comparator audience affinity, the biggest Black Friday demand and documented price gaps. Good second-wave candidates are **perfume and beauty** (many perfume e-shops, cosmetics the top PayU BF category, beauty growing at eMAG) and **books** (ISBN, large spreads). Fashion (variants, decline, Glami and international marketplaces) and groceries (state monitor, flyers) are weaker starts. Affiliate commission rates by category could not be sourced.
