## 1. SRL formation in 2026 and PFA comparison
Covers share capital, ONRC steps, cost and time, registered office, two-founder set-up, bookkeeping, the micro-enterprise employee rule and zero revenue. Ends with a structure recommendation for €0–2k, €2–10k and €10k+ per month.

### Takeaway
- **Share capital.** A new SRL needs at least **RON 500**. The rule comes from Law 239/2025 (Monitorul Oficial no. 1160 of 15.12.2025).
  - RON 5,000 applies once net turnover exceeds RON 400,000.
  - The same law restricts repaying shareholder loans when net assets fall below half of share capital. ANAF fines for this apply from 2027. This matters for founder-financed start-ups.
- **Incorporation.** It can be filed online with ONRC. Commercial sources quote about **RON 152** in ONRC/Monitorul Oficial fees and **3–5 working days** (secondary sources).
- **Registered office.** A founder's flat works under a loan-for-use contract (*comodat*). The owners' association's opinion (*aviz*) is not needed if the administrator declares that no activity takes place there (Law 31/1990 art. 17(6), as stated by ONRC).
- **30 vs 90 days: resolved.** Since **25 Feb 2026 (OUG 8/2026)**, a new company opting for the micro regime has **90 days inclusive** from registration to hire its employee (Fiscal Code art. 48(3)). The old rule was 30 days.
  - If the deadline is missed, the company owes **16% profit tax** (art. 17) from the next quarter.
  - With zero revenue, micro tax is 1% of zero. With zero profit, profit tax is zero too. Losses carry forward 5 years, capped at 70% of later profits (art. 31).
- **PFA in 2026.** Income tax is 10%.
  - CAS (25%) is due once net income reaches 12 minimum wages (RON 48,600, so CAS of 12,150). From 24 minimum wages (RON 97,200) it is 24,300.
  - CASS (10%) is charged on net income: minimum base 6 minimum wages (RON 24,300), cap 72 (RON 291,600).
  - These thresholds use **RON 4,050**, the minimum wage on 1 Jan 2026. The minimum gross wage rose to **RON 4,325 from 1 July 2026** (HG 146/2026).
- **Recommendation (illustrative model below).** For two founders, use an SRL.
  - **€0–2k/month:** use the profit-tax regime (no employee) until the business can carry a minimum-wage contract.
  - **€2k to about €8.3k/month:** a micro SRL with one founder on a minimum-wage contract has the lowest total state take, about 24–33% of pre-tax profit. The ceiling is €100k/year.
  - **€10k+/month:** the micro regime is unavailable and a profit-tax SRL costs about 31–34%. A real-system PFA would cost about 17–27%, but is impractical for two co-owners and carries unlimited liability.

### Cited Findings
**Share capital: Law 239/2025**
- Law 239/2025 amended the Companies Law and was "published in the Official Gazette No. 1160/15.12.2025" — [NNDKP Corporate Legal News, 18 Dec 2025](https://www.nndkp.ro/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/NNDKP-Corporate-Legal-News_December-2025.pdf)
- Thresholds — [NNDKP](https://www.nndkp.ro/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/NNDKP-Corporate-Legal-News_December-2025.pdf):
  - "for companies with a net turnover above 400,000 RON, the minimum share capital is 5,000 RON";
  - "for newly established LLCs, the minimum share capital is 500 RON";
  - a company crossing RON 400k must increase capital "by the end of the following financial year";
  - capital is "not automatically reduced" if turnover later falls;
  - existing SRLs must adjust "within two years", with a 50% Monitorul Oficial Part IV fee reduction;
  - non-compliance can lead to court-ordered dissolution.
- Fiscalitatea reproduces **Art. VI** of Law 239/2025 — [Fiscalitatea, 1 Jan 2026](https://www.fiscalitatea.ro/capital-social-2026-valoarea-minima-a-capitalului-social-conform-legii-2392025-24587/):
  - para. (2) for new SRLs: "valoarea minima a capitalului social este de 500 lei" (*the minimum share capital is 500 lei*);
  - para. (6): increases "nu mai tarziu de 2 ani de la data intrarii in vigoare a prezentei legi" (*no later than 2 years from entry into force*);
  - para. (7): 50% publication-fee reduction for increases made by 31 Dec 2026.
- **Entry-into-force date conflicts:**
  - NNDKP gives publication on 15 Dec 2025;
  - Bono says "în vigoare din 1 ianuarie 2026" (*in force from 1 January 2026*) — [Bono, updated 18 May 2026](https://bono.ro/blog/cum-infiintezi-un-srl);
  - one search snippet said 18 Dec 2025 (snippet).
- **Other Law 239/2025 rules relevant to founders** — [NNDKP](https://www.nndkp.ro/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/NNDKP-Corporate-Legal-News_December-2025.pdf):
  - if net assets fall below half of subscribed capital, the company "cannot repay loans previously granted by shareholders";
  - net assets must be restored, with fines of RON 10,000–200,000;
  - companies that have not restored net assets within 2 years and owe money to shareholders must convert those claims into capital (fine RON 40,000–300,000);
  - these sanctions apply "starting in 2027, with reference to the financial statements for the fiscal year beginning on January 1, 2025, or later";
  - an exemption covers individuals who invested €2,500–200,000 in a micro or small enterprise and hold ≤25%;
  - a controlling shareholder's share transfer is enforceable against ANAF only if notified within 15 days.

**Shareholders and administrator**
- You need "un asociat și un administrator (pot fi aceeași persoană)" (*one shareholder and one administrator, who may be the same person*) — [Bono](https://bono.ro/blog/cum-infiintezi-un-srl). The fuller statutory rules were not verified (see Gaps).
- Micro condition (h): shareholders holding over 25% must designate the company as "singura persoană juridică stabilită de către asociați/acționari să aplice prevederile prezentului titlu" (*the only legal person they designate to apply the micro regime*). Art. 47(4): that designation must be made by 31 March of the following year — [ANAF consolidated Fiscal Code, updated to Law 170/2026](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Anaf/legislatie/Cod_fiscal_norme_2023.htm)

**Micro-enterprise regime: Fiscal Code Title III, in force 27 Oct 2026** — [ANAF consolidated Fiscal Code](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Anaf/legislatie/Cod_fiscal_norme_2023.htm)
- **Art. 47(1)** conditions, tested "la data de 31 decembrie a anului fiscal precedent" (*on 31 December of the previous fiscal year*):
  - (c) revenue not above the lei equivalent of €100,000;
  - (d) capital not held by the state;
  - (e) not in dissolution;
  - (g) "are cel puțin un salariat, cu excepția situației prevăzute la art. 48 alin. (3)" (*has at least one employee, except under art. 48(3)*);
  - (h) the >25%-shareholder designation;
  - (i) annual financial statements filed on time.
- **Art. 47(1¹)(d), linked enterprises.** Revenue of a PFA, II or IF run by a >25% shareholder counts towards the €100k ceiling.
- **Art. 48(3), new companies, as amended.** The company "poate opta … începând cu primul an fiscal" (*may opt for the regime from its first fiscal year*) if (d) and (h) are met at registration, "iar cea prevăzută la lit. g) în termen de 90 de zile inclusiv de la data înregistrării" (*and condition (g) within 90 days inclusive of registration*). Otherwise "microîntreprinderea datorează impozit pe profit începând cu trimestrul următor celui în care expiră perioada de 90 de zile" (*it owes profit tax from the quarter after the 90 days expire*).
  - The previous text said "în termen de 30 de zile inclusiv" (*within 30 days inclusive*).
  - Source for the change: OUG 8/2026, in force from 25.02.2026, published in Monitorul Oficial no. 147 of 25.02.2026 — [ANAF DGRFP Brașov comparison table, row 18](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Brasov/Brasov/legea_227_III.pdf)
- **Other OUG 8/2026 changes** — [ANAF DGRFP Brașov table](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Brasov/Brasov/legea_227_III.pdf):
  - art. 48(3¹): employment suspended for less than 30 days still counts;
  - art. 48(3³): sick leave counts if it totals no more than 30 days a year;
  - art. 52(1): above €100k the company owes profit tax "începând cu trimestrul în care s-a depășit această limită" (*from the quarter in which the limit was exceeded*);
  - art. 52(3): if the only employee leaves, there are 30 days to hire a replacement on an indefinite contract or one of at least 12 months;
  - art. 54(1): the test uses turnover; fixed-asset or land transfers are added only if more than one asset per subgroup is sold.
- **Art. 51(1):** "Cota de impozit pe veniturile microîntreprinderilor este de 1%" (*the micro-enterprise tax rate is 1%*).
- **Art. 51(4)**, definition of employee: a full-time employment contract; or part-time contracts that add up to one full-time equivalent; or "contracte de administrare sau mandat … în cazul în care remunerația acestora este cel puțin la nivelul salariului de bază minim brut pe țară" (*administration or mandate contracts paying at least the national minimum gross base wage*).
- **Art. 48(2):** a company may opt for micro "începând cu anul fiscal următor celui în care îndeplinesc condițiile" (*from the fiscal year after the one in which it meets the conditions*).
- **2026 ceiling in lei:** €100,000 = RON 509,850, at BNR's 31.12.2025 rate of 5.0985 — [ContApp, 31 Jul 2026, updated 1 Sep 2026](https://contapp.ro/blog/plafoane-fiscale-2026-pentru-pfa-ii-si-microintreprinderi/)

**Profit tax and losses** — [ANAF consolidated Fiscal Code](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Anaf/legislatie/Cod_fiscal_norme_2023.htm)
- Art. 17: "Cota de impozit pe profit … este de 16%" (*the profit tax rate is 16%*).
- Art. 31(1): losses from 2024 onwards "se recuperează din profiturile impozabile realizate, în limita a 70% inclusiv, în următorii 5 ani consecutivi" (*are recovered from taxable profits, up to 70%, over the next 5 consecutive years*).

**€0–2k/month**
- Incorporate the SRL anyway. It is needed for:
  - B2B invoicing to 2Performant, Profitshare and Romanian CPC merchants;
  - the art. 317 special VAT code (Q2);
  - a clean legal person for the ANCOM/DSA notification.
- At registration, **do not opt for micro** (or let the 90-day window lapse). Profit tax on near-zero profit costs nothing.
- From the next 1 January, opt for micro (art. 48(2)) once one founder can be put on a minimum-wage contract, or an administrator mandate at the minimum wage or above (art. 51(4)(b)).
- Below roughly €2k/month (the break-even in the model), the salary costs more than the micro-vs-profit-tax saving.
- A single PFA is cheapest at very low income (about 19%), but it is one person's business with unlimited liability.

**€2–10k/month**
- A micro SRL with one employee gives the lowest burden in the model: about 24–33% of P, and the salary goes to a founder.
- Watch the **€100k/year ceiling (about €8.3k/month average)**. It includes linked enterprises, including founders' PFAs (art. 47(1¹)(d)). Crossing it switches the company to 16% profit tax from that quarter (art. 52(1)).

**€10k+/month**
- The micro regime is unavailable. A profit-tax SRL (16% + 16% dividends + capped CASS) takes about 31–34%.
- Two real-system PFAs would take only about 17–27%. But:
  - each founder would have to invoice separately, while affiliate networks and AdSense pay one account per site;
  - liability is unlimited;
  - splitting one business into two PFAs invites requalification under the art. 7 point 3 criteria [inference].
- Keep the SRL and ask an accountant to optimise the salary/dividend mix.

**Two-founder design**
- Two 50/50 shareholders each hold more than 25%, so neither founder can hold another micro-regime company (art. 47(1)(h)).
- Consider capitalising above RON 500, or documenting founder funding as capital rather than loans. With RON 500 capital, first-year losses put net assets below half of capital almost immediately. That blocks repayment of founder loans and triggers the net-asset restoration duty (Law 239/2025; fines from 2027).

**VAT threshold**
- RON 395,000 (about €77.5k). Art. 310(2) counts operations "cu locul în România" (*whose place of supply is Romania*).
- So AdSense and EU network commissions, whose place of supply is abroad (see Q2), should not count towards the threshold [inference from the article text; confirm with an accountant]. Domestic network commissions, CPC fees and B2C subscriptions do count.

## 2. VAT and invoicing mechanics for affiliate and ads income
Covers (a) 2Performant and Profitshare, (b) foreign networks and AdSense (reverse charge, special VAT code, VIES, D390/D301), (c) e-Factura B2B and B2C in 2026, (d) SAF-T D406, and (e) natural persons receiving affiliate payouts.

### Takeaway
- **(a) Romanian networks.** The affiliate SRL issues the invoice itself; this is not self-billing.
  - 2Performant: a non-VAT-registered Romanian company "uploads an invoice issued without VAT".
  - Profitshare: PFAs and companies must issue an invoice and upload it between the 22nd and 25th of the month.
  - Both parties are Romanian taxable persons and the place of supply is Romania, so the invoice must also go through **RO e-Factura within 5 working days** (OUG 120/2021 art. 10(7), as amended by OUG 89/2025 from 1 Jan 2026).
- **(b) Google Ireland (AdSense) and EU-established networks.** B2B services are supplied where the customer is established (Fiscal Code art. 278(2)), and the customer self-accounts for VAT.
  - A non-VAT-registered SRL must obtain a **special VAT code under art. 317(1)(b) *before* supplying**, and under art. 317(1)(c) *before receiving* EU B2B services such as Google Ads or EU SaaS.
  - It must file the **recapitulative statement D390** (art. 325(1)(c) for supplies, (1)(e) for purchases) for each month with such operations.
  - It must file the **special return D301** (art. 324(1)(d), by the 25th of the following month) and pay 21% Romanian VAT on reverse-charged purchases, with no deduction.
  - Supplies to UK or US entities are outside D390 (art. 325(1²) for UK).
  - Invoicing: RO rules apply, with the mention "taxare inversă" (*reverse charge*). If the customer self-bills, as Awin does with "self-billing invoices", the rules of the Member State where the supply takes place apply (art. 319(5)(b)).
- **(c) e-Factura in 2026.**
  - B2B and B2C use the same deadline: 5 working days.
  - B2C reporting has been mandatory since 1 Jan 2025, **including for non-VAT-registered SRLs**. Consumers are identified by CNP, or by 13 zeros if they give no identifier.

## 4. EU guidance texts: Commission 2021 Notices on Art. 6a PID (2021/C 526/02) and the UCPD (2021/C 526/01)

### Takeaway
- **Art. 6a (30-day prior price) does not bind a pure comparator.** The Commission's Art. 6a guidance says Art. 6a "does not apply to the intermediaries … that merely aggregate and display information about the prices provided by other sellers (**price comparison platforms**)". These intermediaries remain under the general professional-diligence and intermediary-liability rules. The PID applies only if the intermediary is the seller or sells on a trader's behalf.
  - Pretoscop's *own* price-advantage claims (e.g. "best price", "−20% vs. typical price", crossed-out reference prices) fall under the UCPD (Art. 6(1)(d)) and must not look like a seller's price reduction.
- **Ranking disclosure.** The UCPD Guidance confirms that **Art. 7(4a)** applies "only to traders that allow consumers to search for products offered by other, third party, traders … i.e. online marketplaces and **comparison tools**".
  - General, non-per-query information on the main parameters and their relative importance must sit in a specific section directly and easily reachable from the results page.
  - It does not cover default (non-query) page layouts.
- **Paid placement.** Under **Annex I point 11a**, any paid inclusion or payment for higher ranking must be clearly disclosed. Recital 20's examples of indirect payment include "**increased commission per transaction**".
  - Disclose next to each affected result with a salient "Ad"-type label. A single prominent statement on the results page is enough only if paid ranking affects all results.

- **Section 1.2 "Traders concerned":**
  - Art. 6a "applies to the trader who is the actual party in the contract with the consumer, i.e. to the seller of the goods, including sellers using intermediaries, in particular online marketplaces."
  - "In contrast, Article 6a does not apply to the intermediaries that provide the mere means for traders to sell their products, such as online marketplaces, or that merely aggregate and display information about the prices provided by other sellers (price comparison platforms). These intermediaries remain subject to the general rules on intermediary liability and professional diligence obligations. The intermediary is, however, subject to the PID rules when it is the actual seller of the goods or when it sells on behalf of another trader."
- **Section 1.1:** Art. 6a "does not apply to general marketing claims that promote the seller's offer by comparing it with other sellers' offers without invoking or creating the impression of a price reduction, such as 'best/lowest prices'". It "also does not apply … to other techniques of promoting price advantages that are not price reductions such as price comparisons". These "continue to be subject to the UCPD".
- **Section 3:** comparisons "with other prices, e.g. prices of other traders or the manufacturer's recommended retail price" are "outside the scope of Article 6a … but remain fully subject to the UCPD".
  - "Where, due to its misleading presentation, the price comparison is actually perceived by an average consumer as a price reduction, such practice can amount to the breach of both the UCPD and Article 6a".
  - Footnote 21: price comparisons between traders also fall under the Misleading and Comparative Advertising Directive 2006/114/EC.

**UCPD Guidance (2021/C 526/01)** — [EUR-Lex 52021XC1229(05)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(05))
- **Section 4.2 (digital sector):** the UCPD applies to "comparison tools". Footnote 337: "Comparison tools is to be understood in a broad manner, covering functionalities on online marketplaces, review tools etc." It also refers to the 2015–2016 multi-stakeholder principles.
- **Section 4.2.1, whether a platform is a trader:** a platform may be a trader when "it charges a commission on the transactions …, provides additional paid services or draws revenues from targeted advertising". Example: "A grocery price comparison service was considered by a national court to be a trader's website and a tool for comparative advertising" (footnote 338: Tribunal de commerce de Paris, 29 mars 2007, *Carrefour c/ Galaec* [sic, Leclerc purchasing cooperative]).
- **Section 4.2.1, transparency:** relevant points include "the coverage of the platform's offer (e.g. sectors and types of and number of suppliers), the frequency of updates of the information (in particular on the price and availability of products), how it selects the suppliers". This helps avoid misleading "'best deal' or 'recommended choice'" labels.
  - "Promoting prices or products where the platform is reasonably aware of the fact that they are not actually available could be in breach of Articles 6 and 7 UCPD and … the black list in Annex I" (points 5, 6, 18).
- **Section 2.9.1 (Material information):** "Some comparison tools use claims such as 'best deals' to identify not necessarily the cheapest deals, but rather those that offer the best value for money. Omitting information about the criteria for the 'best deal' claim could be misleading under Article 7".
- **Section 2.8.2, price advantages:** traders "must pay particular attention to clearly inform the consumer that the indicated reference price is a comparison and not the reduction of the price charged by that trader previously … especially relevant when using … crossed-out reference price". RRPs used in comparisons "should be explained".
- **Section 4.2.3, Art. 7(4a):**
  - it applies "only to traders that allow consumers to search for products offered by other, third party, traders or by consumers, i.e. online marketplaces and comparison tools";
  - it "does not apply to the default organisation of the online interface … that is not the result of a specific search query";
  - the description "can remain at a general level and does not have to be presented in a customised manner for each individual search query";
  - it belongs in "a specific section … directly and easily accessible from the page where the offers are presented";
  - trade secrets cannot justify refusing to disclose the main parameters, but there is no duty to disclose algorithms.
- **Section 4.2.3, Annex I point 11a:**
  - the prohibition text: "Providing search results in response to a consumer's online search query without clearly disclosing any paid advertisement or payment specifically for achieving higher ranking";
  - it "applies to any trader providing for a possibility to search for 'products' … including search engines";
  - indirect payments include "Increased commission per transaction" and "Different compensation schemes that specifically lead to higher ranking";
  - excluded are "payments for general services, such as listing fees or membership subscriptions … provided that such payments are not dedicated to achieving higher ranking";
  - disclosure must be "in immediate affiliation to the relevant search result in a visually salient way". But "where payments … are part of the ranking parameters and influence the ranking of all the results displayed", a "single clear and prominent statement on the search results page" can suffice, in addition to the Art. 7(4a) information.
- **Enforcement examples in the same section:**
  - Booking and Expedia replaced "sponsored" with "'Ad', 'Advert', 'Advertisement', or similar equivalent text in local language".
  - "A price comparison website offered to top rank the products of traders paying an additional fee … the comparison website's commercial practice was qualified as misleading" (Cass. com., 4 Dec 2012, no. 11-27729, *Pewterpassion.com v Leguide.com*).

### Inferences
- Pretoscop may display a retailer's own strike-through discount without being bound by Art. 6a itself.
  - Professional diligence (UCPD Art. 5(2)) and the "reasonably aware" standard suggest it should not amplify discounts its own price history shows to be fake. A "price history / real discount" indicator is a defensible differentiator.
- Pretoscop's own "lowest in 30/90 days", "−X% vs average" or "best deal" badges must state their reference basis and criteria next to the claim, and must not be styled like a seller's markdown.
- If affiliate commission level feeds into ranking, it is both a "main parameter" (Art. 7(4a)) and an "indirect payment" (point 11a). Label "Anunț"/"Promovat" next to each affected result, or keep commission out of ranking.

- How the Romanian ANPC applies point 11a to comparators was not checked.

## 7. Price-alert e-mails: consent, Law 506/2004 art. 12/13, and whether "you asked for this alert" e-mails are commercial communications

### Takeaway
- **Law 506/2004 art. 12(1)** bans commercial communications by e-mail unless the subscriber "şi-a exprimat în prealabil consimţământul expres" (*has given prior express consent*).
  - **Art. 12(2)** allows the soft opt-in only for a customer's e-mail obtained in a sale, for similar products, with an easy, free objection.
  - **Art. 12(3)** requires the real sender identity and a valid opt-out address in every message.
- **Sanctions** sit in **art. 13**. ANSPDCP practice cites **art. 13 alin. (1) lit. q)** for art. 12 breaches. Fines run from 5,000 to 100,000 lei, or up to 2% of turnover for companies above 5 million lei.
- **Price alerts are almost certainly direct marketing.** They promote third-party products with affiliate links. The CJEU's *Inteligo Media* (C-654/23, 13 Nov 2025, a Romanian reference) treats even informative newsletters sent individually for a commercial purpose as direct marketing.
- So "you asked for this alert" is not an exemption in itself. The request for the alert, if captured as specific, express, documented opt-in, *is* the consent.
- Pure service messages (password reset, account changes) are not commercial.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 12 Law 506/2004** — [legeaz.net, art. 12](https://legeaz.net/legea-506-2004-prelucrare-date-caracter-personal/articolul-12):
  - **(1)** "Este interzisă efectuarea de comunicări comerciale prin utilizarea unor sisteme automate de apelare …, prin fax ori prin poşta electronică sau prin orice altă metodă care foloseşte serviciile de comunicaţii electronice destinate publicului, cu excepţia cazului în care abonatul vizat şi-a exprimat în prealabil consimţământul expres pentru a primi asemenea comunicări." EN: *Commercial communications by automated calling systems, fax, e-mail or any other public electronic communications method are prohibited unless the subscriber has given prior express consent to receive them.*
  - **(2)** soft opt-in: an e-mail address obtained directly from a customer "cu ocazia vânzării către acesta a unui produs sau serviciu" (*in the course of selling them a product or service*) may be used for "produse sau servicii similare" (*similar products or services*), "cu condiţia de a oferi în mod clar şi expres clienţilor posibilitatea de a se opune printr-un mijloc simplu şi gratuit" (*provided customers are clearly and expressly offered a simple, free way to object*);
  - **(3)** e-mails that hide the sender's real identity, or give no valid opt-out address, are prohibited "în toate cazurile" (*in all cases*);
  - **(4)** paragraphs (1) and (3) also apply to legal-person subscribers.
- **Art. 13** (older text on legeaz.net; it still names ANRC and the Ombudsman as enforcers, so the lettering is outdated) — [legeaz.net, art. 13](https://legeaz.net/legea-506-2004-prelucrare-date-caracter-personal/articolul-13):
  - the contraventions list includes "nerespectarea prevederilor art. 12, referitoare la comunicările nesolicitate" (*breach of art. 12 on unsolicited communications*);
  - fines are "de la 5000 lei (RON) la 100.000 lei (RON)", and for companies "cu o cifră de afaceri de peste 5.000.000 lei (RON) … până la 2% din cifra de afaceri" (*RON 5,000–100,000; up to 2% of turnover above RON 5 million*).
- **Current lettering (Elefant Online SA):** ANSPDCP fined it 10,000 lei under "**art. 13 alin. (1) lit. q)**" of Law 506/2004 for marketing e-mails after unsubscription. It "nu a făcut dovada obținerii consimțământului prealabil expres și neechivoc" (*could not prove prior express and unambiguous consent*) — [StartupCafe, 15 Oct 2019](https://startupcafe.ro/elefant-ro-magazin-online-amenda-gdpr-htm-11321)
- **Money Seeds (COBI)**, online toy shop: fined €3,000 by ANSPDCP after it "a prelucrat adresa de e-mail a petentului în vederea transmiterii de comunicări comerciale" (*processed the complainant's e-mail address to send commercial communications*) despite an earlier objection. It was ordered to ensure only consenting people receive advertising — [StartupCafe, 8 Jan 2026](https://startupcafe.ro/nu-e-de-joaca-magazin-online-de-jucarii-amendat-cu-3-000-de-euro-pentru-ca-a-trimis-e-mailuri-nesolicitate-unui-client-92629)
- **CJEU *Inteligo Media* (C-654/23, 13 Nov 2025)** — [Matheson](https://www.matheson.com/insights/cjeu-clarifies-direct-marketing-rules-under-eprivacy-directive/):
  - a newsletter sent to free-account users of a Romanian news portal "qualifies as 'direct marketing'" even though informative, because it pursues "a commercial purpose" and is "addressed directly and individually to a consumer";
  - a free account can be a "sale" for the Art. 13(2) soft opt-in, since a sale "does not require direct monetary payment";
  - where Art. 13(2) is met, "Article 6(1) GDPR does not apply";
  - the soft opt-in "must be interpreted strictly".
  - Bird & Bird also stresses the narrow, model-specific reading — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/understanding-soft-opt-in-when-free-deals-count-as-consent-under-eprivacy-rules) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Treat price-drop alerts, back-in-stock alerts and deal digests as commercial communications.** For each alert type:
  - get an unticked, specific opt-in when the user sets the alert, e.g. "Vreau să primesc e-mail când prețul scade" (*I want an e-mail when the price drops*);
  - confirm it with double opt-in;
  - log the time, IP, wording and source.
- **Every alert must carry** the SRL's identity and an easy unsubscribe link, ideally one-click per alert and global (art. 12(3)).
- **The soft opt-in (art. 12(2))** might extend to logged-in users of a free account tied to a premium upsell, following *Inteligo*. It is narrow, so use it only as a fallback, not the main basis.
- **Retention.** Keep consent records for as long as alerts are sent plus the limitation period. ANSPDCP's practice (Elefant) turns on the operator proving consent.
